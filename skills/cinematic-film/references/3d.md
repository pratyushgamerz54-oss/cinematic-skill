# 3D shots, motion graphics and clips

The board stays the film's one world. 3D shots, motion-graphics inserts and moving footage enter it as
**clips**: videos that play inside a print on the wall, or fill the frame for a cutaway when the camera
pushes into them. Their timing is set by the voiceover like everything else.

| What | Made with | Enters the film as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3D globe: countries filling in, great-circle routes, labelled markers, a camera flight | `make_globe.py` (three.js template in `assets/globe/`), recorded by `frame-capture` | `Clip` |
| Any other 3D scene: a city, fort, battlefield terrain, ship, weapon, machine | the `threejs-scene` skill (or `exploded-view` for a cutaway), recorded by `frame-capture` | `Clip` |
| Web motion graphics (GSAP, CSS, canvas, SVG charts) | any page, recorded by `frame-capture` | `Clip` |
| HyperFrames compositions (titles, kinetic type, charts, map overlays, transitions, ~450 registry blocks) | the `hyperframes` skills, `hyperframes render` | `Clip` |
| Remotion compositions (React) | the `remotion-*` skills, `remotion-render` | `Clip` |
| Archive film, your own footage, AI video clips | a file in `assets/clips/` (`motion-assets` plans AI clips in one style) | `Clip` |
| 2D motion graphics on the board | engine items: `Timeline`, `BarChart`, `Counter`, `BigText`, `Card`, `PenMark`, `Route`, `RegionFill` | items |

`threejs-scene`, `frame-capture`, `motion-assets` and `exploded-view` come from Open 3D Skills
(https://github.com/Moh4696/open-3d-skills). In this repo they are installed under `.claude/skills/`.

## The 3D globe

1. Write `globe.json` in the project (see `scripts/make_globe.py --help`): places, fills (country lists
   with a colour and a start time), routes, markers with labels, camera keys. Times are seconds into the clip.
2. Check single moments: `make_globe.py --project P --still 4.5`. Then a timing pass: `--draft` (8 fps).
3. Record: `make_globe.py --project P`. It writes `assets/clips/<name>.mp4` and adds it to `clips.json`.
4. `prep_clips.py --project P` extracts the frames at the film's frame rate.
5. In `scene.py`: `film.add(Clip(doc.clip("<name>"), x, y, w, t_in=L(3), frame=False))`.

Camera keys: `lat`/`lon` is the point the camera looks down on; `dist` is the distance from the centre in
earth radii (5 shows the whole globe, 2.2 a region, 1.6 a country); `tilt` (degrees) swings the camera south
and aims at the surface, for a horizon view; `roll` leans the frame. Keys ease in and out.

Looks: `night` (space, stars, blue atmosphere) cuts well against the dark wall; `board` (brown, no stars)
sits inside a wall film as if it were part of it. Labels use the project's `fonts/label.ttf`, so Hindi
and other Devanagari labels work.

## Clips in the scene

```python
from engine import Clip
globe = film.add(Clip(doc.clip("empire_globe"), 0, -14000, 7680, t_in=L(1), frame=False))   # a bare screen
film.shot(L(1) + 0.2, 0, -14000, 0.5)                                                       # fill the frame: zoom = 3840 / w
film.add(Clip(doc.clip("ships"), 9000, 2000, 2400, rot=1.5, t_in=W(9, "ships"), label="ARCHIVE FILM, 1936"))
```

- **Full-frame cutaway:** a `frame=False` clip of width `w` fills a 16:9 frame at camera zoom `3840 / w`.
  Push in from the board to the clip, hold, pull back out: the cut feels like the camera flying into a screen
  on the wall. Keep the spotlight off it (a big `focus` radius) while it fills the frame.
- **On the wall:** `frame=True` (the default) gives it the print border, pin, drop-in and red name bar.
- `start` (seconds into the clip), `rate` (playback speed), `loop=True`. Past its end a clip holds the last frame.
- Clips have no sound. Put any sound they need in the cue list with `film.cue(...)`.
- Grade clips like the photos (`clips.json` `grade`: natural, bw, sepia) so they read as one set.
- Frames are read one at a time from `assets/prep/clips/<id>/`, so long clips cost disk, not memory.

## Recording any three.js or motion-graphics page

Follow `frame-capture`: serve the page with its `serve.py` (Range requests), make the page take time from
`performance.now()` or `window.__recordFrame(t)`, set `window.__ready = true` once loaded, and record with
`record.py URL out.mp4 --seconds S --fps 30 --size 1920x1080 --ready "window.__ready === true"`.
Record at the film's frame rate (30) so frames map one to one.

Headless machines without a GPU render WebGL in software (SwiftShader). It is correct but slow: a 10 s
1080p globe takes several minutes. Do the `--draft` pass first. If Playwright's own Chromium is not
installed, set `CHROME_PATH` to a Chromium binary (`make_globe.py` tries `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`).

## 2D motion graphics on the board

- `Timeline(x, y, w, [(date, caption, t), ...], t_in)`: an axis draws on, events pop on in turn and a red
  marker rides to the newest. Good for "thirteen years earlier..." bridges and a reign at a glance.
- `BarChart(x, y, w, h, [(label, value), ...], t_in, suffix=" लाख", highlight=1)`: bars grow one after
  another with values rolling up. Use it for one comparison that matters (empire sizes, armies, costs).
- With `Counter`, `BigText`, `Card`, `PenMark`, `Route`, `RegionFill`, `StringLine` and `Stamp` these
  cover lower thirds, data reveals, map animation and investigation-board marks.

Keep rule 1 in mind: one hero per line. A chart, a globe and a photo on the same line fight each other.
